\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

The application of complex-network theory to financial markets traces to
Mantegna~\cite{mantegna1999hierarchical}, who showed that the MST of an
equity-correlation matrix reveals a hierarchical market structure
dominated by industry classification, and to Mantegna and Stanley's
\emph{econophysics}~\cite{mantegna1999introduction} framing of
cross-asset co-movement.  Bonanno et~al.~\cite{bonanno2001topology}
confirmed that the MST length distribution across time contains
predictive information about regime shifts, while Onnela, Chakraborti,
Kaski and Kert\'esz~\cite{onnela2003dynamics} demonstrated that the
dynamics of market correlations exhibit scale-free clustering on the MST.
Tumminello, Aste, Di~Matteo and
Mantegna~\cite{tumminello2005tool} generalised this framework into a tool
that recovers correlation-based networks at multiple time scales via the
Planar Maximally Filtered Graph (PMFG).

A complementary strand investigates the empirical deficiencies of the
MST and PMFG as sparse approximations: Aste, Di~Matteo and
Tumminello~\cite{aste2010correlation} showed that the information content
of correlation filtering depends strongly on the topology of the backbone.
Squartini, Van~\hbox{Lint} and
Garlaschelli~\cite{squartini2018early} used MST and PMFG networks as
inputs to early-warning signals for systemic risk in interbank networks.
On the community-detection side,
Newman~\cite{newman2003structure,newman2006modularity} introduced the
modularity-$Q$ objective that we adopt here, and Blondel, Guillaume,
Lambiotte and Lefebvre~\cite{blondel2008fast} introduced the greedy
modularity-maximisation algorithm widely known as Louvain, which scales
gracefully to networks with millions of edges and is the algorithm we
deploy on the top-$k$ graph.

Methodologically, our contribution is closest to Brida and
Risso~\cite{brida2010dynamical}, who applied the MST+Louvain framework
to the Latin-American Integrated Market (MILA), and to
Podosu~\cite{podosu2013network}, who applied it to the Romanian BET.
NepGraph extends this line to NEPSE and, unlike these studies, performs
an explicit \emph{pre-registered} $k$-sweep that calibrates the top-$k$
sparsification parameter from data, rather than adopting it heuristically.

A parallel literature develops topology-based anomaly detection on
correlation networks~\cite{fiedor2014information, bardoscia2015guide,
lillo2002econophysics}.  NepGraph contributes to this strand by defining
a simple, transparent sector-mismatch anomaly score on top of the
Louvain partition.  The score is deliberately conservative: it requires
a minimum of $50\%$ of the top-$k$ MST neighbours to come from a
different official sector before flagging, which keeps the
false-positive rate low while surfacing the most economically
interesting cross-sector links.

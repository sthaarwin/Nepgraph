\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We present \textbf{NepGraph}, an open-source analytical framework designed to uncover the true topological structure of the Nepal Stock Exchange (NEPSE) from historical price data, challenging the adequacy of its official sector classifications. By transforming almost 23~years of daily closing prices (June 2003 to April 2026) for a 124-instrument universe into a Pearson correlation matrix, we convert similarities into a distance metric $d_{ij}=\sqrt{2(1-\rho_{ij})}$ and apply the Louvain algorithm to a top-$5$ sparsified nearest-neighbour graph. We empirically justify $k=5$ as the mathematical \emph{sweet spot} that preserves global market connectivity without fragmenting the graph into disconnected islands, yielding a highly modular structure ($Q=0.66$).

Crucially, we find that the market's organic community structure deviates significantly from NEPSE's official sector labels (Adjusted Rand Index $\text{ARI}=0.21$). The network topology proves that investors trade based on hidden relationships that actively fracture monolithic official sectors into distinct, independently co-moving clusters. Furthermore, centrality analysis (Spearman $\rho \approx 0.08$ between eigenvector and betweenness) reveals contrasting structural roles: Commercial Banks act as the connective tissue bridging the broader market, whereas Hydro Power stocks operate as isolated, tightly-knit cliques. We exploit these structural divergences to develop a \emph{sector-mismatch anomaly score} that surfaces stocks moving incongruously with their official sector. Validated by an edge-swap null model ($z=47.0$, $p<10^{-3}$) and rolling 1-year stability tests, our findings demonstrate that data-driven communities provide a far more accurate map of systemic market behaviour and diversification than traditional administrative sectors.
\end{abstract}
